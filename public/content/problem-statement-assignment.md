# Pre-work: Define a School Challenge

## Purpose

Before the workshop, identify one challenge from your school or teaching context. You will use this challenge to explore how a simple web app might help.

Focus on understanding the problem before proposing a solution. You do not need to know yet whether an app can fully solve it.

Set aside 10–15 minutes. Choose a situation you have seen more than once, rather than a one-off inconvenience. A clear problem statement will make the next PRD step much easier.

## Problem Statement Template

> **[WHO] struggle(s) to [SPECIFIC TASK OR PAIN] when [SITUATION / CONTEXT] because [ROOT CAUSE].**

## What Each Part Means

- **WHO:** The person or group experiencing the problem. You may include what they are trying to achieve.
- **SPECIFIC TASK OR PAIN:** The specific task they find difficult or the problem they experience.
- **SITUATION / CONTEXT:** When or where the problem occurs.
- **ROOT CAUSE:** The underlying reason the problem occurs. Describe the cause, not a proposed solution.

## A Simple Way to Build It

1. **Start with a real moment.** Think of a recent lesson, meeting, or routine that felt unnecessarily difficult.
2. **Name the person affected.** Be specific: “new homeroom teachers” is clearer than “teachers.”
3. **Describe the friction.** What are they trying to do, and what makes it difficult in that moment?
4. **Look one layer deeper.** Ask “why does this keep happening?” A root cause is usually a missing process, scattered information, competing constraints, or a communication gap.

## Stronger vs. Weaker Statements

**Too broad**

> Teachers struggle with technology.

**More useful**

> [New homeroom teachers] struggle to [find the right family-communication template] when [they need to respond to a concern quickly] because [templates and prior examples are spread across several places].

The stronger version identifies a person, a concrete task, a context, and a cause. It gives you something you can investigate and design for later.

## Example

> **[Elementary school teachers who want to create a comfortable classroom where students interact positively with their peers] struggle to [create effective seating arrangements] when [changing seats] because [they need to consider several factors at once, such as students’ individual needs, peer relationships, and classroom dynamics].**

## Before You Submit

- Describe one specific challenge from your school or teaching context.
- Explain the situation in which the challenge occurs.
- State the underlying cause as clearly as you can.
- Do not include a proposed app or tool as the problem itself.
- Do not include real student names, photos, or other sensitive personal information.

## Final Check

Before publishing, read your statement once and ask:

- Could someone outside my school understand who has the problem?
- Does it describe a current difficulty rather than the app I want to build?
- Could I observe or ask someone about this situation during the workshop?
- Have I removed names or details that identify students or families?
